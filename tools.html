Tools

Classical number decoders

Three tools for reading numbers the way Sanskrit texts wrote them — and one lookup for the naming syllable. All three read from the units place upward, under the rule aṅkānāṃ vāmato gatiḥ.

Bhūtasaṅkhyā

Word-numerals. Hyphenate the words; the value is read units-first.

ākāśa 0vasudhā 1nabhas 0kara 2

2010

Try — the obliquity constant in Sūrya Siddhānta II.28.

Kaṭapayādi

Consonant code of Kerala. Each consonant carries a digit; read right to left.

n 0ṭ 1n 0r 2

2010

is the chronogram that closes the Nārāyaṇīya.

Nāmākṣara — the naming syllable

The classical rule is a lookup, not a sum: the Moon’s nakṣatra and pada select a sound. Enter a sidereal longitude (0–360°) to see which.

Syllable

cu

Nakṣatra: Aśvinī (1 of 27)

Pada: 1 of 4

Regional tables differ for two or three nakṣatras — Śravaṇa and its neighbours among them. Where a family has an almanac, that almanac takes precedence.
