The reading

A number is only worth what its source is worth.

Chaldean numerology reached London in 1926 as a book of numbers with a worked example on the page — John Smith, nine and eight, compound seventeen, root eight. That example is the reason this platform can be checked: the arithmetic is printed, so the engine can be held against it, and it is, in the test suite, on every build.

The Indian form keeps seven of those assignments and replaces two: Uranus and Neptune give way to Rāhu and Ketu. The substitution is stated by the author in his own words, which is why the platform prints it as a substitution rather than a discovery.

The Lo Shu square is Chinese, and late to Indian practice. It is labelled as Chinese everywhere it appears here, because a square that sums to fifteen on every line is a fact about arithmetic, not a fact about the Vedas.

What the platform will not do is print a figure whose rule it cannot name. The digit-to-planet pairing has no classical Sanskrit citation that we could find, so the page where it is used says exactly that.
