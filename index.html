IWhat the numbers are made of

Three traditions, named separately, never blended.

01

Chaldean arithmetic

Letter values, the compound numbers 10–52, single-digit reduction. Verified against the worked example in the book itself — John Smith, 9 and 8, compound 17, root 8.

Cheiro · London 1926
02

Graha-numerology

Nine digits for nine planets. The Indian form keeps seven of Cheiro’s assignments and replaces Uranus and Neptune with Rāhu and Ketu — a substitution the source states in its own words.

Johari · Delhi 1990
03

The Lo Shu square

Placement, plane lines, missing and amplified digits. Chinese in origin, entering Indian practice late, and labelled as Chinese everywhere in this platform.

Chinese · 4th c. BCE

IIInside the engine

Deterministic, and open to inspection.

One pure function takes a name and a date and returns everything — three core numbers, the grid, the friendships, the timing cycles, the reading and the provenance list.

  • Mūlāṅka, bhāgyāṅka, nāmāṅka — compound and root kept separately
  • Lo Shu grid with the standard placement, planes, missing and amplified digits
  • Harmony triangle, lucky elements, gemstone, mantra, direction
  • Kua with both century branches and the eight directions
  • Bhūtasaṅkhyā, kaṭapayādi and the 108 nāmākṣara syllables

IIIWhere the platform stops

It will not print a figure whose rule it cannot name.

The digit-to-planet pairing has no classical Sanskrit citation — none has been found in the texts examined. So the platform says so, on the page where the pairing is used, rather than dressing a 1926 London table in Vedic robes.
