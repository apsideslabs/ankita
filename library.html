What the engine is made of

Five books and four notation systems. Each entry carries the date it entered the tradition and the licensing position of the copy that was read.

Book of Numbers

Cheiro (William John Warner) · London, 1926

Chaldean letter values; the compound numbers 10–52; digit-to-planet table with Uranus at 4 and Neptune at 7.

The letter table was verified against the book’s own worked example — John Smith → 9 and 8 → 17 → 8.

Public domainverified

Numerology: With Tantra, Ayurveda, and Astrology

Harish Johari · Destiny Books, 1990

Psychic, name and destiny numbers; the Indian digit-to-graha table; planetary friendships; projection into the future.

Johari states the Rāhu/Ketu substitution in his own words, and notes that the origin of his “Vedic Square” is unknown in India.

In copyrightverified

Aṅk-Vidyā (Jyotiṣa)

Gopesh Kumar Ojha · Motilal Banarsidass; reprints 1966–1982

Indian practice of the 1970s: bhāgyāṅka, nāmāṅka, and the integration of numerology with lagṇa, bhāva and daśā.

Contains no Lo Shu grid and no Kua number — a useful negative control.

The Lo Shu square

Chinese tradition · 4th c. BCE onward

The 3×3 placement, plane lines, missing and repeated digits.

Labelled Chinese throughout the platform. It is not Vedic and is not presented as such.

Four classical notations

These are offered as decoders. None of them assigns a meaning to a number; all four exist so that a number can be written inside a verse.

SystemEarliest evidenceHow it worksUsed for
BhūtasaṅkhyāPiṅgala, Chandaḥsūtra (c. 2nd c. BCE)Digits carried by ordinary words — moon 1, eye 2, fire 3, Veda 4 — read units-first.Decoding numbers embedded in Sanskrit verse.
