How each number is made

Three traditions, kept apart. Each rule below names its source and its status: documented, attributed, or an inference of this platform.

01

Mūlāṅka

The birth day reduced to one digit. Born on the 29th: 2 + 9 = 11 → 2. The reduction is ordinary digital-root arithmetic.

Documented · Cheiro, 1926
02

Bhāgyāṅka

Every digit of the full date summed and reduced: 29-08-1995 → 2+9+0+8+1+9+9+5 = 43 → 7. The Indian name for the same operation the West calls the life path.

Documented · Ojha, Aṅk-Vidyā
03

Nāmāṅka

Chaldean letter values, each word reduced and the words summed — Cheiro’s own order of operations. John Smith: 9 + 8 = 17 → 8, the compound 17 being printed separately.

Documented · Cheiro, ch. XIII
04

Digit to graha

Nine digits for nine planets. 4 is Rāhu and 7 is Ketu in the Indian form; Cheiro assigned Uranus and Neptune. The substitution is stated by Johari in his own words.

Documented · Johari, 1990
05

Lo Shu square

Every non-zero digit of the date, plus the bhāgyāṅka, plus the mūlāṅka when the birth day is 10 or more. The name number is never placed, and the Kua number is not placed.

Chinese · labelled as such
06

Kua

Both century branches: for births from 2000, men reduce 9 − Y and women 6 + Y. Kua 5 defaults to 2 for men and 8 for women.

Chinese · compass school
07

Vimśottarī daśā

Computed from the Moon’s sidereal longitude: the nakṣatra gives the lord, the fraction traversed gives the balance, and the nine lords total 120 years.

Classical · from the Moon
08

The 45-year cycle

One year for 1, two for 2, to nine — summing to 45. It is arithmetic, not a classical daśā, and it is labelled a modern cycle wherever it appears.

Modern · labelled

IIWhat is not claimed

Where the tradition runs out, the platform says so.

The digit-to-planet pairing has no classical Sanskrit citation that we could find in the texts examined. The reading layer — nature, strengths, watch-outs, careers — is a summary of the modern literature, marked attributed, not verse. The platform does not print predictions of events.
