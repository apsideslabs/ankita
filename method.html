Method

Three layers, six corrections

A numerology engine is a stack of borrowed rules. This page states which rule came from where, and what had to be fixed before any of it could be coded honestly.

011926

Chaldean arithmetic

Letter values, compound numbers 10–52, single-digit reduction. Verified against the book’s own worked example.

021990

Graha-numerology

Nine digits for nine grahas. The Indian form keeps seven of Cheiro’s assignments and replaces Uranus and Neptune with Rāhu and Ketu.

03Chinese

The Lo Shu grid

A 3×3 square of 1–9 summing to 15 in every line. Labelled Chinese, because that is its origin.

The six corrections

Each of these is a place where a widely circulated formula breaks, and each is fixed in the engine.

Commonly saidWhat holds
Kua: male 11 − Y, female 4 + YThat pair is the 1900s formula only. Births from 2000 need a different pair of constants, so Aṅkita branches on the century and applies the Kua 5 defaults.
“Vedic dasha”: 1 year for 1 … 9 for 9The arithmetic is right — the sum is 45 — but it is not a classical daśā. The classical systems run from the Moon’s longitude. Aṅkita labels it a modern cycle.
Lo Shu line names: Rāja yoga, Silver yogaThe lines are correct for the grid as drawn. The names are modern English coinages; no jyotiṣa text calls a completed diagonal a rājayoga.
“9 is sacred and omitted from letters”Correct, and Cheiro gives the reason: nine letters spell the name of God. It is his reason, not an Indian one.
Master numbers 11, 22, 33A Pythagorean convention. Aṅkita offers it as a toggle and reduces to 1–9 by default, because nine numbers serve nine grahas.
Compound numbers “10 through 52+”Cheiro stops at 52 and says why — the 52 weeks of the year. There is no 53.

What is verified

  • · Chaldean letter values — reproduced from a worked example in the source.
  • · The compound range 10–52 — stated by the author, with his reason.
  • · Digit → graha, Indian form — stated by Johari as a variant of the Western table.
  • · Bhūtasaṅkhyā, kaṭapayādi, Āryabhaṭa notation — dated texts, dated usage.

What is not

  • · A classical Sanskrit citation for digit-to-graha. None has been found.
  • · Any claim that the Lo Shu grid or the Kua number is Indian.
  • · Any predictive claim. The engine computes; it does not promise.
  • · The regional pada tables — two or three nakṣatras vary by tradition.
